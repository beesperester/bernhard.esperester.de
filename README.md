# bernhard.esperester.de

A tiny Game Boy Color–style garden. Every visitor is a cat.

- Game Boy style, three buttons (`src/game/controls.ts`): **Arrow keys** move, tap one twice to dash · **Space** (A) jump · **X** (B) paw: tap to catch / splash / paw at a tree, hold to crouch and creep, let go to pounce (a short low leap onto the nearest prey within about three cat lengths, two for a kitten; it uses the dash, so it needs the dash rested, else it's a swipe) · **E** (SELECT) mew; hold it and ← hiss, → roll · **?** or the ? button shows all the keys on a wooden sign. On touch: D-pad, A, B and SELECT
- Dash lasts while the double-tapped direction is held (max 2.5 s); recovery takes as long as it was used, ×12 (a full dash needs 30 s, at least 2 s)
- Koi drift about out in the open water. Stand at the shore and press **X** to slap the water: on calm water the splash may make one curious (best at dawn and dusk, and where there's plenty of open water; slapping again and again only keeps them away), and it swims over to wait a little way off. Now hold **X** to crouch and keep still: it creeps up to your paws and noses about there for a few seconds — swipe again to catch it. Splash again too soon, get up and walk, hiss or jump, and it's gone (and the water has to calm down before the next one comes)
- Beetles (ladybirds, rose chafers, and black ground beetles at night) crawl about the lawns and paths; they can't fly, so a scared one skitters off into the grass. By day dragonflies dart about over the ponds, hovering here and there; catch one while it hovers low.
- Butterflies, bees, birds, frogs, beetles and dragonflies all keep an eye out: walk, jump, hiss or dash at them and they're off. Hold **X** to sneak closer; at about two cat lengths they spot you (**!**) and leave a moment later, so freeze and let go of **X** to pounce before they do (dashing into any prey but fish grabs it). Catching a bee gets you stung: hiss!
- Bigger prey is worth more on the scoreboard: butterflies, fireflies and beetles 1, dragonflies 2, fish, frogs and mice 3, little birds and bats 4, a blackbird 5; a bee sting costs 1
- Dashing through grass startles a burst of butterflies, and every dash leaves a little cartoon puff of cloud where the cat shot off
- Press **X** in mid-jump to swipe at whatever flies within reach: a bird taking off (jump at one pecking on the lawn and grab it as it rises), a bat swooping low, a butterfly; the cat eats it on landing
- People have left things in the park: a garden shed, park benches, a picnic table, a bistro set, a picnic blanket with a basket, and a bird bath. Birds love the bath: by day many of them come to drink and splash about on its rim, where they can be stalked like birds on the lawn. Jump up onto the benches and tables; the shed hides cats behind it
- **Climb the trees:** jump up into a tree and from branch to branch to its top. Branches are jumped through from below and landed on from above; walk off the end of one or press **↓** to drop to the one below (hold it to climb all the way down). Broadleaf trees zigzag up from side to side and some branches only start further out, which takes a well-aimed jump. Pines go up in tiers of boughs (and stay green all winter). Some trees have a bird house on the trunk, and the big tree by the meadow has a tree house with a deck and a thatched hut. Curl up on a bird house or in the tree house and your dash is ready again.
- Birds drop in on the trees, sit on their branches and sometimes flutter down to peck on the lawn. Walk up and they fly off (sneak along a branch to catch one up a tree). Hold **X** to crouch and creep closer: at about two cat lengths the bird spots you (**!**) and leaves a moment later, so freeze and let go of **X** to pounce before it does (creeping on or a missed swipe scares it away)
- **Day and night follow the real sun over Berlin**: sunrise and sunset come at the actual times for today's date. The garden's top faces south: the sun rises on the left, stands at the top at noon and sets on the right, so shadows fall left, down and right. The light warms at dawn and dusk, and at night the park turns deep blue. By day, everything standing up (cats, trees, bushes, rocks, hedges, fences, cliffs) catches a sunlit rim on the side facing the sun, so you can tell where it is. Trees, bushes and rocks have no lighting painted in: each pixel keeps a surface normal and is shaded from where the sun stands, so their lit sides turn from left in the morning to right in the evening (and go soft, lit from above, at night). The sun rays fall in from the sun's side too. Shadows follow the sun too: terrain, hedges, bushes, rocks, fences and the shed are raycast against the sun over a height map of the garden, while trees, lanterns, furniture and cats lay their outlines on the ground, long in the morning and evening and short at noon (a cat standing in shade darkens). After sunset they give way to soft round shadows under everything. Lanterns along the paths light up at dusk, and stars and the moon (in its real phase) shine in the ponds
- After dark there are no butterflies or bees; fireflies drift over the long grass instead (catch them with **X**), birds sleep up in the trees, frogs sit at the water's edge or out on the lily pads (they hop back in if you come too close; catch and eat them like fish), bats flit round the lanterns and over the ponds or hang upside down under a tree for a rest, field mice hide in the tall grass and bolt for the next tuft when a cat comes through, or scurry about the lawns and paths (catch one in the open), owls glide in to sit on a tree top or a lantern and look about with glowing eyes (hiss or jump at a bat or an owl and it flies off) and cats' eyes glow. Add `?time=21:30` to the URL to see another time of day, or `?speed=600` to run the day faster
- **The seasons follow the Berlin calendar**, changing gradually over the weeks:
  - **Autumn:** from late September the leaves turn yellow, orange and red and come drifting down until late November. They lie on the lawns and in piles under the trees, which burst when a cat runs or pounces through them. The long meadow grass goes to hay.
  - **Winter:** the trees stand bare from December to April (the pines keep their needles, under snow). From mid-December to the end of February the garden is under snow (lawns, paths, hedges, plateaus, branches, furniture, the shed roof) and the ponds are frozen. You can walk out on the ice, but it's slippery: speed builds slowly, turns are wide and the cat slides on after you let go (dash for a long slide). No fish, frogs or reflections while it's frozen. Every cat leaves paw prints in the snow.
  - **Spring:** the cherry trees blossom in April and drop their petals, the apple tree flowers white in May (and has red apples in late summer), and the wildflowers come up through spring and fade in autumn.
  - **Animals keep their seasons:** butterflies and bees April–September, dragonflies mid-May to mid-September, fireflies June–July, frogs mid-March to October (fading out into early November), bats mid-April to mid-October, beetles April–October; birds and owls stay all year (but nobody bathes in a frozen bird bath).
- **Weather:** now and then a shower or a wet afternoon passes over, at any time of year. The drops splash on the ground and ring the water, the light goes grey, shadows and sun rays fade, and you hear the rain. In winter it snows on and off instead. Rain and snow are random spells from the shared garden clock (not real weather), so everyone in the garden has the same weather. Add `?date=2027-01-15` to see another day of the year (with its sun and season), and `?weather=rain` or `?weather=snow` (or `none`) to make it rain or snow right away
- **Fruit and squirrels**: the trees bear in their season (acorns and hazelnuts in autumn, cherries in early summer, apples in late summer, pine cones all year) and drop it now and then; paw at a trunk (C) to shake something down. Red squirrels live in some trees: by day they come down to hop about, anything that lands near their tree brings one down to fetch it and sit nibbling, and a cat that comes too close sends it scurrying back up. On the ground a squirrel can be caught. Fruit that falls by itself and the squirrels' outings follow the shared garden clock; a pawed-at tree is sent to the other cats, and what falls follows from the tree and the moment, so everyone sees the same (`src/world/fruit.ts`, `src/game/fauna/Squirrels.ts`)
- **Rivers and streams flow**: the Spree and the canals run the way the real ones do (their direction comes from OpenStreetMap), and small streams run from their higher end to their lower one, with glints of light drifting along. Whatever falls in floats along with the current, round the bends and under the bridges: leaves in autumn, cherry petals in spring (the same leaf for everyone, on the shared clock). Running water never freezes: in winter it keeps flowing between the frozen ponds (`src/world/streams.ts`)
- Frogs come out in a good shower too, by day as well as at night
- **Puddles** collect in the dips of the paths (and here and there on the lawn) while it rains: the deeper ones first, growing the longer it pours, ringed by the falling drops, and drying up over an hour or so afterwards (the same for everyone, from the shared clock). Walk through one and the cat splashes; dash through or land in one from a jump and the water flies
- **The water** mirrors the sky: blue with drifting clouds by day, warm at dawn and dusk, grey in the rain, dark at night, through small moving waves that mirror more where they tilt away from the eye (Fresnel), with the sun glinting off them. Under the surface, the bed shows through in the shallows (sand and pebbles at the bank, patches of weed, mud) and is lost in murk further out; the fish swim between the two. Lily pads float, so it is as deep under them as round them (`src/art/water.ts`, drawn by `src/game/WaterSurface.ts` a few patches per frame)
- Only your own cat shows through trees (a see-through hole) and hills (a green silhouette); other cats are hidden behind them like anything else
- **Every garden is new**: the landscape is fixed (the Spree and the canals, the lakes, the bridges, the main roads, the terraces and the monuments, Schloss Charlottenburg among them), but the first cat into an empty garden plants a seed, and from it grows everything else: where the lawns, meadows, flower beds and box hedges are, the trees, bushes and rocks, the maze and the tree house, and the furniture. Every cat that joins is handed the seed and gets the same garden, so a garden lives on as long as cats keep coming and going. When the last cat leaves, it keeps the seed for ten minutes: back in time (to an empty garden) and it's the same garden again; otherwise the garden is gone, and the next one is different. (The woods still grow thickest where Berlin's real parks are.)
- **Growing up**: every cat starts out a kitten, small and all head, with a little hop for a jump and a short dash that ends in a tumble; it can only manage bugs, butterflies and the food lying about. At 100 snacks (in all gardens together) it is a young cat, a size bigger, and gets to choose its name: now it jumps up onto rocks and into the trees, and can catch mice, frogs, fish and birds that sit still. At 500 it is fully grown: pouncing, luring fish with a splash, shaking the trees, and catching birds and bats on the wing. A bee sting takes a point off the score, never a size off the cat (`src/game/growth.ts`).
- **Things to find**: explorers eat best. A garden has food left about that counts for more than any prey: lunch in the picnic baskets on the tables and blankets, a pretzel, a currywurst or an ice cream dropped by a bench or on a path, an angler's bucket with the catch still in it by the water, and birds' nests full of eggs, hidden in the bushes and box hedges or up on the branches of the trees. The baskets, snacks and buckets are in plain sight; a nest only shows when your cat is right by it (from a little further off you may catch a glint where it is). Paw at it with **X**. Once eaten it's gone for a while, then there's more (a fresh lunch, a new catch, another snack dropped somewhere along the paths, a new clutch of eggs, every eight minutes or so): snacks 6–8, a basket 7–8, a fish 8, a nest 9, a nest up a tree 12
- **Snacks eaten**: the scoreboard (top right) shows how much each cat in the garden has eaten in its life; bigger prey counts for more, a bee sting costs a point. Each cat's face is shown next to its name. Every cat gets a random cat name on its first visit, unique in its garden. At 100 snacks you can give your cat a name of your own. There is no server: your cat's score and name live in your browser, in a chain of its catches the other cats check (see *How it works*), so they outlast reloads, closed tabs and empty gardens
- **Your collar tag**: the help sign (?) can copy your cat's collar tag, a code to paste into another browser ("Wear a collar tag") to play the same cat there, score, name and all. Whoever has the code is your cat, so keep it to yourself
- The browser tab's icon shows how many cats are in your garden right now (you included)
- Walking straight into an edge slides you into an opening just beside it (corner slip, as in the Zelda games), so the narrow gaps of the labyrinth are easy to enter
- Idle cats sit down after a few seconds and lick their paws now and then; after 30 s they curl up and sleep (zzZZzz)
- Jump onto low obstacles — hedges, bushes, rocks, the fountain rim — and walk along them. Jump into the pond and you splash and scramble straight back out
- **The park is Berlin inside the S-Bahn Ring** (120×90 tiles, one tile ≈ 117 m): the Spree, the canals, the lakes, the main roads and their bridges keep their real layout, the Ring is a path round the edge, and the rest is lawns, meadows, flower beds and woods (thickest where the real parks are). Invented terraces with stairs, garden ponds with lily pads, a hedge labyrinth (too tall to jump) with a fountain, the tree house, benches and tables are placed around that. Ten landmarks stand where the real ones are as weathered, mossy garden monuments (Fernsehturm, Brandenburger Tor, Reichstag, Siegessäule, Berliner Dom, Rotes Rathaus, Gedächtniskirche, East Side Gallery, Checkpoint Charlie, Oberbaumbrücke): some whole, some fallen into ruin with their stones scattered about, and most of them can be climbed ledge by ledge. Paw at one (C) to read its plaque
- Background music and sounds start with your first key press or tap; the ♪ button mutes the music, the speaker button the sounds; both pause while the tab is hidden. Birds sing now and then from a tree near you; cats boing when they jump, purr when content, hiss, mew, whoosh off when dashing, patter along paths (lower on the wooden bridges, brighter on stone stairs), rustle through tall grass and chew their catch, and the water splashes — for every cat near you
- The corners button toggles full screen. iPhones can't take a web page full screen, so there the button explains how to add the garden to the home screen, which opens it full screen (`public/manifest.webmanifest`).
- On touch devices an on-screen D-pad and buttons appear

## How it works

- **Phaser 4 + TypeScript + Vite**, built to static files.
- **All art is drawn in code** (`src/art/`): sprites are character maps, recoloured per coat; the garden ground is generated from the park map in `src/world/garden.ts`, whose characters are explained in `src/world/legend.ts` (one entry per kind of tile) and drawn by `TILE_ART` in `src/art/tiles.ts`.
- **The map is generated** from OpenStreetMap data by `tools/berlin/generate.ts` (`sh tools/berlin/fetch.sh` downloads the data once into `tools/berlin/cache/`, which is not committed); it writes `src/world/garden.ts` (the fixed part of the park: the tile rows with the water, paths, bridges and stairs, which parts are park, wood or town, terrain levels, landmark spots, river lines and spawn box) and a preview PNG. What grows on it is laid out in the browser from the garden's seed (`src/world/layout.ts`; the things to find in `src/world/finds.ts`): the first cat in a garden makes the seed, every cat already there hands it to a newcomer (who waits behind the loading bar to hear it before building anything), and should two gardens with different seeds meet, the younger one starts over in the older. Edit the generator and run it again rather than editing `garden.ts`. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Each monument is a few simple shapes (blocks, columns, balls, domes) in `src/world/landmarks.ts`: the same shapes are what cats stand on and what `src/art/landmarks.ts` draws, so a ledge that looks reachable is.
- **The UI is pixel art too** (`src/ui/pixel.ts`): text boxes, buttons, the d-pad and the music and sound icons are drawn in code and handed to the CSS as image variables. The text is set in [Pixelify Sans](https://fontsource.org/fonts/pixelify-sans) (SIL Open Font License), bundled with the site rather than loaded from Google Fonts.
- **Height map** (`src/world/map.ts`): every tile has a terrain level (16px each, more than a jump) plus whatever stands on it; cats only move onto surfaces no higher than they are, so jumping (real gravity) gets them onto hedges but not up a cliff. Walking climbs up to 3px, which is what makes stairs work. Tree trunks block only at their base, up to the fork.
- **Terrain** (`src/art/terrain.ts`): each row of raised ground is cut from the ground image and drawn lifted by its height, with a stone retaining wall below and depth-sorted like everything else; a cat that ends up behind a hill shows through as a solid green silhouette where the hill covers it.
- **Trees**: `src/world/trees.ts` plans every tree from its map tile (`T` broadleaf, `P` pine, `H` the tree house) as a list of perches, one-way platforms at a height that cats land on from above (`surfaceAt`, used by the movement, the scene and the anti-cheat witness alike); `src/art/trees.ts` draws each tree to match its plan; `src/game/Trees.ts` shows a cat behind one through a round dithered window in the foliage. A cat up a tree is drawn in front of it.
- **Atmosphere** (`src/game/`): `Meadow` sways ~600 grass/flower/reed sprites in a rolling wind and bends them away from cats; `Sunlight` overlays dithered god rays fanning in from the sun's direction that breathe and dim under passing clouds, plus dust motes; `Lighting` re-shades the trees, bushes and rocks from their normals (`art/lighting.ts`) whenever the sun has turned a step; `Clouds` drifts soft dithered cloud shadows across the ground; `PondReflection` mirrors nearby cats, trees and reeds onto the water with a ripple (split into chunks, only those on screen are redrawn); `Vignette` softly darkens the screen edges. Static things off screen are skipped by `Culler`.
- **Fauna** (`src/game/fauna/`): one module per animal family (fish, bees, butterflies, birds, fireflies and frogs, bats and owls, beetles and dragonflies, mice), all behind one small interface and listed in `fauna/index.ts`; what each kind of prey is worth and how it's eaten is in `fauna/prey.ts`; splashes, feathers, Zs and dust are pooled in `fauna/Effects.ts`. `Ambient` runs them all and settles catches. To add an animal: write a module, list it, give new prey an entry in `PREY`.
- **Cat movement** (`src/game/movement.ts`): speeds, the dash and its cooldown, stepping up, corner slip and jump height, free of Phaser so it is tested and used by the anti-cheat checks.
- **Networking is peer-to-peer** (`src/net/net.ts`) via [Trystero](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero): visitors find each other through public Nostr relays, then exchange cat states directly over WebRTC. The web server only serves files.
- **Fauna and clouds are synced without traffic**: peers agree on a shared garden clock (a few request/response pings to the lowest peer id), and every butterfly, bee, bird, fish and cloud is a pure function of that clock and a seed. The only fauna message is "fish/butterfly/bee/bird N was caught"; birds and butterflies taking flight are worked out from the (synced) cat states.
- Each cat broadcasts `[x, y, z, facing, act, seq, coat, mews]` (`mews` counts its mews, so a cat can mew while walking, sneaking, dashing or jumping) ~10×/s while something changes (20×/s mid-jump), otherwise once per second.
- A garden holds 20 cats; later visitors are moved into `garden-2`, `garden-3`, …
- **Signed, so nobody speaks for another cat** (`src/net/identity.ts`): every browser makes itself a key pair on the first visit (ECDSA P-256 from the browser's own crypto, kept in IndexedDB; it only leaves on a collar tag, `src/net/collar.ts`), and a cat's id is the hash of its public key. A cat signs what it says about itself (its scoreboard entry, bound to its current connection, and its catches): a changed byte breaks the signature and the statement is dropped. Signatures can't stop a cat lying about itself, which is what the checks below are for
- **Fair play without a server** (`src/net/witness.ts`, `src/net/consensus.ts`, `src/game/Referee.ts`): every visitor checks every other cat against the rules: no faster than a dash (with leeway for network jitter), no more dashing than the cooldown allows, never inside a fence or a trunk, no higher than a jump, not floating. A rule broken is a strike; three within a minute and that visitor accuses the cat to everyone. A cat that most of the others accuse is ignored for the rest of the visit (its states, catches, votes and scoreboard entry are dropped). Catches are put to a vote too: each visitor says whether it saw that prey within the catcher's reach just then; a catch most of them turn down doesn't count (the catcher doesn't vote; a background tab abstains).
- **A ledger of catches, like a cryptocurrency's** (`src/net/ledger.ts`, `src/game/Board.ts`): each cat keeps its own chain of signed blocks, one per catch (the garden's seed, the prey and its id, the points, the time, and the hash of the block before), in its browser (`src/net/chainStore.ts`), one chain per cat as in Nano's block-lattice. Its score is what the chain adds up to. Cats tell each other the head of their chain, and whoever hasn't seen it asks for the blocks it's missing and checks every one: signature, link, that the prey exists and is worth that, that no prey is claimed twice (like a coin spent twice), and that catches don't come quicker than a cat can eat (a second apart, at most 400 points in ten minutes); for other cats, a cat's score is what its chain checks out at, and how far each chain checked out is remembered, so a cat coming back only has its new blocks checked. Cats that played alongside another for a minute co-sign the head of its chain; co-signatures weigh by how much the co-signer has eaten itself (a quarter for a newcomer, a whole for a grown cat, so a second browser of your own vouches for little), and a whole's worth makes a checkpoint, after which newcomers are sent only the blocks since. Catches nobody else saw can't be checked yet; replaying the cat's moves is planned for that

## Music

There are two playlists: every `.mp3`/`.ogg`/`.m4a` in `src/music/day/` plays while the sun is up, and those in `src/music/night/` after dark, following the garden's Berlin sun (the file name is the title). Songs play in random order, never the same one twice in a row; with a single song it loops. At dusk and dawn the current song fades out and the other playlist takes over; if one folder is empty, the other plays day and night. Convert masters before adding them, e.g.

```sh
ffmpeg -i "Song.wav" -c:a libmp3lame -q:a 2 "src/music/day/Song.mp3"
```

The original WAVs (music and sounds) are kept in `masters/`, laid out like `src/` and named like their converted files; the folder is ignored by git, so it only exists on the machine they were added on.

## Sounds

Every folder in `src/sounds/` is a sound group (`birds`, `purr`, `hiss`, `mew`, `dash`, `splash`, `eat`, `rustle`, `steps`, `jump`, `levelup` (growing up a size), `rain` — a seamless loop that plays while it rains, louder the harder it pours; later e.g. `wind`), and each play picks one of its files at random (never the same twice in a row). Groups play on Web Audio, so any number overlap.

- **Ambient layers** (`LAYERS` in `src/audio/sound.ts`) fire on their own at random intervals with random volume and pitch, e.g. `birds: { every: [12, 40], volume: [0.35, 0.6], rate: [0.94, 1.08] }`. The scene can say where they come from (`sound.setSource('birds', …)` picks a tree near you).
- **Game sounds**: `sound.play('hiss', { at: { x, y } })` — sounds placed in the park fade with distance from your cat and pan left/right. `loop: true` returns a handle to `stop()` or `setVolume()` later (for wind or purring).

- **Cat sounds** (`src/game/CatSounds.ts`) are worked out from the synced cat state, so they play for other cats too.

Short effects are best as mono MP3, peak-normalised to -3 dB so the volumes in code are comparable (here for a file peaking at -12 dB):

```sh
ffmpeg -i "birds.wav" -ac 1 -af volume=9dB -c:a libmp3lame -q:a 3 src/sounds/birds/birds-2.mp3
```

## Development

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm test           # unit tests (Vitest)
npm run build      # typecheck, tests, → dist/
node tools/preview.ts /tmp/preview   # render sprite sheets & garden to PNG
```

## Deployment

GitHub Pages: every push to `main` builds the site and publishes `dist/` (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`), served at https://bernhard.esperester.de. The domain points at GitHub with a DNS record at all-inkl: `bernhard` → CNAME → `beesperester.github.io`. No server-side code is needed; the cats talk to each other directly.
